Yes. This is the point where we should stop treating TrustScore as merely a “NIN verification product” and design the **actual company/product around what NIN makes possible**.

I researched the current NINAuth/NIMC ecosystem, its enterprise and wallet model, the 2026 NIMC Act position, Nigerian data-protection requirements, and the competitive KYC/fraud landscape. The biggest strategic opportunity is clear:

> **NIN should be TrustScore's identity root. TrustScore's proprietary value should sit above it: identity assurance, verified credentials, trust profiles, reputation, dispute resolution, transaction-specific trust decisions, and eventually a network of trust signals.**

NINAuth itself is now positioned by NIMC as more than a basic NIN lookup: it supports online identity verification, QR/share-code flows, consented data sharing, digital identity, and a wallet that can contain government and third-party credentials.  The 2026 NIMC Act also explicitly positions NIN as Nigeria's foundational identity credential and NIMC as central to the country's digital identity, PKI and DPI infrastructure.

# 32. SECURITY MODEL

NINAuth's current developer documentation specifically requires secure OAuth handling, PKCE and backend-only client secrets. It also instructs applications to validate returned ID tokens rather than merely decoding them.

Therefore:

```text
Frontend
  ↓
NINAuth
  ↓
Authorization code
  ↓
Backend
  ↓
PKCE validation
  ↓
Token exchange
  ↓
ID token validation
  ↓
Trust Identity
```

Never:

```text
Mobile App
  ↓
NINAuth client secret
```

# 33. DATA PROTECTION MUST BE DESIGNED IN

The NDPA gives data subjects rights around access, rectification, objection, erasure, portability and automated decision-making. Section 37 specifically addresses solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.

NDPC guidance also identifies scoring/profiling, significant automated decisions, sensitive/highly personal data and other high-risk processing as circumstances requiring DPIA consideration.

Therefore:

### TrustScore V1 must include

```text
Privacy Center
Consent Center
Data Access
Data Correction
Deletion Request
Appeal
Human Review
Decision Explanation
```

from the beginning.

There is one direction I would actively prevent the team from taking:

> **“Let's collect as much NIN information as possible and build a giant identity database.”**

That is not the opportunity.

NINAuth itself emphasizes user-controlled sharing, and its current retention policy states that NINAuth's underlying identity records remain within NIMC-authorized infrastructure rather than being independently owned by NINAuth.

TrustScore should therefore strive for:

**verified evidence without unnecessary possession of raw identity data.**

That is both a better security architecture and a stronger product proposition.

## The product should have three layers

### Layer 1 — **Identity**

Powered by NIN/NINAuth.

> Who are you?

### Layer 2 — **Evidence**

Powered by credentials, phone, biometric verification, verified interactions and dispute outcomes.

> What can be established about you?

### Layer 3 — **Trust**

Powered by our own reputation/risk/decision engine.

> What does the evidence reasonably imply for this situation?

That separation is the **central architecture and product principle**.
